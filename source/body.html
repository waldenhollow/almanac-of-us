The idea

There is one day when you are exactly the age your mother was when you were born.

On that same day she is exactly twice your age, and has spent exactly half her life as your mother. It happens once, and no calendar marks it. This one does, along with every other day like it hiding in your family.

What we count

Twelve kinds of day that no calendar prints

Each one is plain arithmetic on birthdays, and each one happens to a family exactly once.

Echo Day
A child reaches the exact age their parent was on the day the child was born. On that day the parent is exactly twice the child’s age, and has been a parent for half a lifetime.
Half-Life Day
You have known someone for exactly half your life. From the next morning on, you have had them longer than you were without them.
Century Day
Two people’s ages add up to exactly one hundred.
Triple Day, Tenfold Day
The single day one person is exactly three, ten or a hundred times as old as another. A new father is a thousand times his daughter’s age a week or two after she is born.
Add-Up Day
The children’s ages add up to exactly their parent’s.
Myriad Day
Ten thousand days, alive or together. It arrives at 27 years and about four and a half months.
Billion Day
One billion seconds, alive or together: 31 years and about eight months.
Kilodays
Every thousand days. One comes round every two years and nine months or so, which means it never lands on the same date twice.
Planet birthdays
A birthday each time another planet finishes a lap of the Sun since you were born. The first on Jupiter comes just before you turn 12, and the first on Saturn at 29 and a half.
A thousand moons
A thousand lunar months, reached a few weeks before the 81st birthday.
Golden birthday
Turning 14 on the 14th, or 30 on the 30th: the one birthday when age and date agree.
Overtake Day
The day the dog or the cat becomes older than you, by the vets’ rule of thumb for animal years.

Questions

Fair things to ask

How exact are the dates?

Exact to the day. Ages are counted in whole days from each birthday, the way a calendar counts them. The few days that depend on the hour of birth, such as a billionth second, say so beside the date.

Where do my family’s birthdays go?

Nowhere. Everything is worked out inside your browser and there is no server behind this page. If you make a link to send, the names and dates are written into that link, so only share it with the people it is for.

Is it really free?

Yes. No payment, no account, no ads. The code is open for anyone to read or reuse.

Where do my family’s birthdays go?

Nowhere. Everything on this page is worked out inside your browser. If you buy the almanac and switch on the calendar link, the names and dates are encrypted into the link itself and our server forgets them. There is no database of families to lose.

What if someone has died?

Tick “no longer with us” beside their name. The almanac stops counting their days and keeps only the ones that are about you, such as the day you reach the age your father was when you were born.

Can I give one as a gift?

Yes. Build the almanac for their family, then send the link or print the year’s edition. They open it without signing up for anything.

Is it a subscription?

No. You pay once and the calendar link keeps rolling forward by itself.

Why is it called an Echo Day?

It had no name, so we gave it one. You arrive at the exact age your parent was at the moment you arrived, one generation later.
